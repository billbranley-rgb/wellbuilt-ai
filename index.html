Why K1

Every dollar in construction waits on something: a photo, an inspection, a sign-off, a lien release. K1 turns verified field evidence — captured, validated, and sealed in the Evidence Vault — into the same record that pays, finances, and settles the job.

How K1 works

Five steps. One unbroken record.

Every K1 evidence item moves through the same five-step path. Each step is logged, attributed, and verifiable on its own — together they form chain-of-custody that holds up outside the jobsite.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Photos, video, drone, robot, and sensor data come in from the field — tagged with project, location, and operator at the moment of capture.

    Field Mode · Modules · Sensors
  2. 02

    Validate

    A supervisor reviews the item against the work in front of them. Nothing reaches the vault without human approval — that's what makes it controlled construction.

    Supervisor approval gate
  3. 03

    Hash

    Approved evidence is cryptographically fingerprinted with SHA-256. Any future tampering breaks the hash and is detectable instantly.

    SHA-256 · timestamped
  4. 04

    Vault

    Sealed records land in the Evidence Vault — immutable, anchored, and tied to the project's spine: RFP, Estimate, Bid, Live Job, Closeout.

    Evidence Vault · anchored
  5. 05

    Prove

    When a delay claim, defect, or insurance event lands, K1 produces a court-ready package — every linked item, every approval, every hash.

    Court-ready export
Evidence Vault

The vault is the point.

Most jobsite software is built to make today easier. K1 is built so the record of today survives every dispute, audit, and insurance review that comes after it.

  • Immutable storage. Once a supervisor seals an item, it cannot be edited, replaced, or quietly removed.
  • Cryptographic chain-of-custody. Every event — capture, approval, link, export — is signed and timestamped.
  • Court-ready export. Counsel gets a package they can hand to an arbitrator, expert, or judge without reformatting.
  • Infinity Curve. The more linked context an item has — RFIs, daily logs, sensor data, approvals — the stronger it scores.
