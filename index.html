How K1 works

Five steps. One unbroken record.

Every K1 evidence item moves through the same five-step path. Each step is logged, attributed, and verifiable on its own — together they form chain-of-custody that holds up outside the jobsite.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Photos, video, drone, robot, and sensor data come in from the field — tagged with project, location, and operator at the moment of capture.

    Field Mode · Modules · Sensors
  2. 02

    Validate

    A supervisor reviews the item against the work in front of them. Nothing reaches the vault without human approval — that's what makes it controlled construction.

    Supervisor approval gate
  3. 03

    Hash

    Approved evidence is cryptographically fingerprinted with SHA-256. Any future tampering breaks the hash and is detectable instantly.

    SHA-256 · timestamped
  4. 04

    Vault

    Sealed records land in the Evidence Vault — immutable, anchored, and tied to the project's spine: RFP, Estimate, Bid, Live Job, Closeout.

    Evidence Vault · anchored
  5. 05

    Prove

    When a delay claim, defect, or insurance event lands, K1 produces a court-ready package — every linked item, every approval, every hash.

    Court-ready export
Evidence Vault

The vault is the point.

Most jobsite software is built to make today easier. K1 is built so the record of today survives every dispute, audit, and insurance review that comes after it.

  • Immutable storage. Once a supervisor seals an item, it cannot be edited, replaced, or quietly removed.
  • Cryptographic chain-of-custody. Every event — capture, approval, link, export — is signed and timestamped.
  • Court-ready export. Counsel gets a package they can hand to an arbitrator, expert, or judge without reformatting.
  • Infinity Curve. The more linked context an item has — RFIs, daily logs, sensor data, approvals — the stronger it scores.

Post-quantum-ready

Built for the post-quantum era.

Large-scale quantum computers are expected, on long-enough horizons, to threaten the public-key cryptography that secures most of today's digital records. Construction, infrastructure, finance, retainage ledgers, BIM/IP, and multi-party payment records routinely live for decades — well inside the window where quantum-aware design matters. K1 is engineered for that horizon.

NIST-aligned roadmap

Standards-track cryptography

Our cryptographic roadmap tracks the NIST post-quantum standards finalized in 2024 — including ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key establishment and ML-DSA (FIPS 204) for digital signatures — alongside SHA-2/SHA-3 hashing and additional standards as they mature. Hybrid classical + post-quantum modes are the default migration path.

Quantum-resistant anchoring

Evidence that survives the horizon

The Evidence Vault is designed for quantum-aware anchoring: hash-based seals, signature agility, and a path to post-quantum signatures so sealed records remain verifiable across the long lifecycles of critical-infrastructure projects.

Long-duration verification

Decade-scale chain-of-custody

Nuclear, semiconductor, defense, and major capex programs need records that hold up 20, 30, 50 years downstream. K1's archive-and-verify model is built around long-duration project verification rather than today-only proofs.

Government & critical infrastructure

Aligned with federal direction

U.S. federal guidance (NSM-10, OMB M-23-02, CNSA 2.0) directs critical-infrastructure operators and vendors toward post-quantum cryptography over the coming years. K1's regulated-vertical coverage — nuclear, semiconductor, defense, corrections — is built to meet customers where that guidance lands.

What this unlocks for the financial stack

  • Tokenized construction payment rights that remain cryptographically credible across the secondary-market lifetime of the underlying receivable.
  • Smart escrow and milestone billing gated by evidence whose integrity is designed to outlast the project, not just the build phase.
  • Retainage and receivables financing backed by long-lived, quantum-aware seals — important when retainage can sit for years.
  • Lien waivers, payment authorization, and surety reporting rooted in a record built to hold up under future cryptographic scrutiny.
  • Long-term evidence vault integrity for BIM, IP, change-order history, and capex audit trails across multi-decade asset lives.

K1 makes no claim that current public-key cryptography is broken today, or that any specific blockchain or ledger will fail. The position is simpler: long-lived records deserve a cryptographic roadmap that anticipates the horizon, and K1's architecture and patent estate are explicitly built for that.
